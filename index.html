Mount Everest

-Asia 8,848m, 29,029 ft

Rise Above, Explore Beyond.

Inquiry

Mount Everest Expedition

Full 45-day guided expedition with permits, logistics, Sherpa support and oxygen systems. Mount Everest, known by the Nepalese / Sherpas as Sagarmatha and by the Tibetans as Chomolungma, is the world's highest peak at 8848m (29,029 ft) and is the border between Nepal and Tibet. Since the 1920s, many of the world's top climbers have attempted to scale Mount Everest, and May 29, 1953, marked the first successful ascent by Tenzing Norgay (Nepal) and Sir Edmund Hillary (New Zealand). SKY PEAK Mountaineering offers the premier climbing experience on Mount Everest in regards to summit success, safety, and overall experience. Our teams work together, have fun, and form lasting bonds of friendship over the course of the expedition. Our camaraderie, as well as the very high level of support, sets us apart from the other teams, we often come to the aid of other climbers in distress. We achieve this with small teams, top guides & sherpas, a focus on safety and good decision making, an overall goal of summit success and making the 'return trip,' and enjoying the experience of Everest throughout the expedition.

Mount Everest Expedition

$65,000

Duration: 45 Days

Difficulty: Extreme

Day 1-10: Acclimatization

Day 11-30: Rotation climbs

Day 31-45: Summit push

  • General Guidance

  • Physical Conditioning: Build endurance gradually; start with smaller hikes to acclimatize and assess fitness levels.
  • Emergency Planning: Inform someone of your planned route and expected return time. Register your number with emergency services if necessary.
  • Equipment: Carry a first aid kit, headlamp, sun protection, and a multi-tool/knife.
  • Safety & Risk Management: Guides are trained to handle emergencies, navigation, and analyze weather risks.
  • Technical Skills: They provide coaching for rock, ice, or glacier travel.
  • Do Not Panic: If lost or injured, stay calm. Use a whistle (3 short blasts) or bright clothing to signal for help.
  • Call for Rescue: Use 112 (Europe) or local mountain rescue numbers. Do not wait until the situation becomes dire.

Mount Everest Hazards & Safety

Understanding the risks, shelter strategies, and emergency responses while climbing the world's highest peak.

Avalanche Hazards

Mount Everest climbers face serious avalanche risks due to unstable snow and ice movement, especially in the Khumbu Icefall.

Extreme Weather

Sudden storms, freezing temperatures, and powerful winds can make climbing conditions extremely dangerous.

Altitude Sickness

Low oxygen levels at high altitude can cause severe altitude sickness, requiring proper acclimatization.

Sheltering

Climbers rely on high-altitude camps and insulated tents to survive freezing temperatures and storms.

Emergency Response

Professional rescue teams and helicopters are essential for evacuating climbers during life-threatening situations.

Safety Equipment

Specialized gear like oxygen tanks, harnesses, and ice axes help climbers manage extreme conditions.

Inquiry

K2 Expedition

Climbing K2 is much more challenging and far more dangerous than climbing Everest via the standard North or Southside routes, and for this reason, we plan to keep our team size small and comprised of qualified climbers, supported by some of the best climbing Sherpas and mountain guides in the world. Unlike on Everest, because the weather and route conditions are notoriously fickle on K2, we must be prepared to take advantage of very brief periods of good weather. Rockfall and snow avalanches are common on the route following K2's Abruzzi ridge, making it often unsafe to climb or camp (as evidenced by the 2013 tragedy), and the monolithic ice cliff which overhangs the "bottleneck" and "traverse" portions of the route on summit day sheds ice frequently (as evidenced by the 2008 tragedy), so taking advantage of good route conditions and moving quickly are paramount.

K2 Mountain Hazards & Safety

K2 is known as the "Savage Mountain". Climbers face extreme technical challenges, harsh weather, and life-threatening risks.

Avalanche Danger

K2 has steep slopes where avalanches frequently occur, making climbing extremely risky for mountaineers.

Extreme Weather

Sudden snowstorms and powerful winds can trap climbers on the mountain for hours or even days.

High Altitude

Low oxygen levels above 8000 meters can cause altitude sickness and serious breathing difficulties.

Sheltering

High altitude camps provide temporary shelter where climbers rest, recover, and prepare for the summit push.

Emergency Rescue

Rescue missions on K2 are extremely difficult due to steep terrain and harsh weather conditions.

Climbing Equipment

Specialized gear like ropes, crampons, oxygen cylinders, and ice axes are essential for survival.

Makalu Expedition

We are very excited to offer Makalu, the world's 5th highest mountain. At just under 28,000 ft., Makalu is located only twelve miles from Mt. Everest in Nepal. The climb is technical, involving steep snow, ice, and rock at high altitude. We begin in Kathmandu, then fly to Lukla to acclimate for a day. Another flight to Tumlingtar takes us into the Makalu region, where we begin the 8-day trek to base camp. Upon arriving base camp we organize loads and begin setting lines to our high camps, acclimating as we go higher, then returning to base camp to rest and evaluate the weather window for a summit attempt.

Often considered the easiest and most accessible of the 8000m peaks, Cho Oyu is a great way to experience high altitude climbing in the Himalayas surrounded by the mystique and intrigue of Tibet. Cho Oyu is located along the Nepal/Tibet border and we access the mountain from the Tibetan (Northern) side. Joining us will be our top climbing Sherpas who also frequently climb with us on Everest, K2, and assist in establishing the route and setting our camps.

Ama Dablam Expedition

Ama Dablam presents a significant climbing challenge with sustained technical sections of snow, ice, and rock at relatively high-altitude with significant exposure. Trekking through the beautiful Khumbu valley and interacting with the local Sherpa culture combine with this challenging climb to create a truly remarkable experience.

Ama Dablam, or "mother's necklace" is an iconic peak located in the Khumbu Valley of Nepal, and was first climbed in 1961. At 6812m / 22,349ft, 'Ama' is a challenging climb because of the steep faces of snow, ice, and rock that one must negotiate during the ascent of the Southwest Ridge. Climbers must have experience with fixed ropes, and be comfortable climbing along very exposed ridgelines.

Carstensz Pyramid, one of the least climbed of the "7 Summits" is a formidable objective located in Papua, Indonesia. Our expedition begins in the beautiful island of Bali, where we fly to Timika then onward to the Yellow Valley base camp. Our high-end logistics, local staff, and mountain guides ensure you are well taken care of throughout the expedition. The mountain is limestone that rises from the thick forest below, jutting to just over 16,000'. This rock climb requires basic rock climbing ability to mostly 5th class terrain on fixed ropes.

Inquiry

SKI The Last Degree Expedition

Ski the last degree of latitude (111 km / 60 nautical miles) to reach the South Pole and experience the challenge and sense of discovery that the first explorers faced to reach the furthest point south. During this polar expedition, you will be immersed in the vast beauty and tranquility of the high polar plateau, and share the camaraderie of your team as you haul sleds and build camps on your way to the Pole. Antarctica's interior is very dry, cold, and windy, with average temperatures around 17F (-8C) when the wind is not blowing the days can feel warm. With the 'physiological' altitude approaching 3350m/11,000ft, we build acclimatization during the week of skiing to the Pole.

Essential Experience:
- Cross-country skiing
- Prior experience towing a sled on cross-country skis
- Multi-day snow camping
- Experience in cold temperatures
